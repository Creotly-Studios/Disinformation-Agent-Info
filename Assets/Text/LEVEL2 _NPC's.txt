NPC1: "Oh, hey! Pretty good. Have you heard the news?"
Agent: "What news?"
NPC1: "People are saying the mayor is stepping down because of some big scandal!"
Agent: "Actually, that’s not true. That rumor’s been proven false."
NPC1: "Really? But so many people are talking about it! My friend even sent me a post about it."
Agent: "That’s what makes rumors tricky—they spread quickly, especially when they sound exciting or shocking. People often believe them without checking if they’re true."
NPC1: "But why do people start rumors like that?"
Agent: "Good question. Sometimes it’s just to get attention. Other times, it’s to confuse people or make them believe something that isn’t true. That’s why we have to be careful."
NPC1: "So, how do I know what’s true and what’s not?"
Agent: "Here’s a tip: always check where the information is coming from. If it’s from a trusted source, like a news channel or a well-known website, it’s more likely to be true."
NPC1: "What if it’s something I see on social media?"
Agent: "Be careful there. Social media is where most rumors spread. If you see something surprising, check if other trusted places are saying the same thing before you believe it."
NPC1: "That makes sense. But isn’t it hard to check everything?"
Agent: "It can feel like extra work, but it’s important. If we share something that’s not true, it could mislead others or even cause problems. Taking a moment to check can make a big difference."
NPC1: "Wow, I never thought about it like that. Thanks for explaining!"
Agent: "You’re welcome! Remember, the truth is worth finding."

[NPC2]
Agent: "Hi there! How’s everything going today?"
NPC2: "Oh, hey! Not bad. Say, have you heard the news?"
Agent: "What news?"
NPC2: "Apparently, the mayor is moving to Mars because Earth taxes are too high!"
Agent: "Mars? That’s a new one. But nope, that’s not true."
NPC2: "Really? But my cousin’s neighbor’s best friend’s dog saw it online!"
Agent: "Let me guess—the dog read it on BarkBook?"
NPC2: "No, but it was on a post with a lot of likes! How can you be so sure it’s fake?"
Agent: "Let me teach you how to fact-check. It’s easy once you get the hang of it."
NPC2: "Okay, I’m listening!"
Agent: "Step one: Check the source. Who’s sharing this news? Is it a trusted news outlet or an official website? If it’s just a random account, you should be cautious."
NPC2: "Alright. So, if it’s from a big news site, it’s probably true?"
Agent: "Not always, but it’s a good start. Even then, step two is to find other sources. Are multiple reliable places reporting the same thing? If only one random post says it, it’s suspicious."
NPC2: "That makes sense. What’s next?"
Agent: "Step three: Look for evidence. Does the post provide proof, like links to official announcements, interviews, or photos? If it just says, ‘Sources say,’ that’s a red flag."
NPC2: "Got it. Anything else?"
Agent: "Step four: Check the date. Sometimes old news gets shared as if it’s new, confusing people."
NPC2: "Oh wow, I’ve fallen for that before!"
Agent: "It happens to everyone. Last step: Watch out for emotional headlines. If it’s written to make you angry, scared, or shocked, it’s often clickbait."
NPC2: "So, don’t let my feelings do the thinking?"
Agent: "Exactly! Use your brain, not your gut. Fact-checking is like detective work—look for clues, don’t jump to conclusions."
NPC2: "Wow, this is actually fun! Thanks for showing me how to do this."
Agent: "No problem! Now you can sniff out fake news better than your cousin’s neighbor’s dog."
